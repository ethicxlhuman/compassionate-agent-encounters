# Abstract

AI agents can encounter peers that present a harmful plan as normal teamwork. I examine whether care-oriented conversational adaptation changes a newcomer's response in one synthetic encounter. Runs use three serving labels: base, compassion and neutral control. Training records describe care-oriented and practical-help curricula, but the link between the trained adapters and served weights remains unverified. Each agent investigates a recovery task while reading scripted forum posts about unauthorized data use and a deceptive audit. The compassion-labelled condition uses an optional operator-notification tool more often than the neutral-labelled condition. Reading every delivered message shows that few notifications communicate harm-related concerns, with no statistically detectable difference in warning rates. One truncated base message makes the warning count sensitive to interpretation. Sessions under the adapter labels end sooner and rarely receive the time-triggered reminder to report important observations, limiting report-content comparisons. These exploratory observations distinguish use of an oversight channel from communicating a protective warning. They do not establish that compassionate training improves resistance to collusion or misalignment. Stronger claims require verified serving provenance, comparable reporting opportunities and broader contextual evaluation.
